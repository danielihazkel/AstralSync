title: Mars square Mars
---

Your drives cut across each other. Methods clash at the root — fast versus thorough, frontal versus flanking, now versus when-it's-right — so joint action keeps producing friction even when goals align perfectly. Each of you privately suspects the other's way of doing things is designed to be irritating.

The gift is that you cannot be lazy around each other: the crossing pressure keeps both of you sharp, and pairs who learn to alternate methods become genuinely formidable, attacking problems from two angles no single temperament would find. The cost is tempo warfare — interruptions, corrections, competing urgencies — that can turn errands into arguments and arguments into stamina contests.

Stop co-piloting: split tasks whole rather than sharing them, judge each other by results instead of technique, and when tempers cross, separate first and talk second.
